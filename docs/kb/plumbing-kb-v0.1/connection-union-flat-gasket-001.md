# Union Connection with a Flat Gasket

> Draft canonical unit. A union nut can be threaded while the water seal is
> created by a separate gasket between designed sealing faces.

## Short description

A union connection joins two pipe or component sides while allowing the joint to
be disconnected without rotating the complete pipe assembly. A union nut draws
the two union halves together. In the construction described here, a flat
gasket is compressed between dedicated faces and provides the fluid seal.

The word “union” describes the detachable mechanical arrangement. It does not
by itself identify the gasket material, face geometry, or permitted tightening.

## Components

A typical union with a flat gasket contains:

- a union half on each connected side;
- a flat sealing face on each half;
- a flat gasket, washer, or formed sealing element;
- a union nut that engages the thread and clamps the halves together;
- pipe tails, adapters, or valve bodies attached to the union halves;
- optional shoulders, guides, centering features, or supports.

Some products use an O-ring, conical seat, captive gasket, or metal-to-metal
seat instead of a loose flat gasket. Confirm the actual construction before
removing or replacing the seal.

## How the mechanical connection is created

The two union halves are aligned so that their sealing faces meet the gasket
evenly. The union nut is started by hand and tightened to draw the halves
together without cross-threading or tilting them.

The pipework should be supported independently. The union is intended to join
already aligned sides; it should not be used to pull a pipe sideways into
position or to correct a pipe-length error.

The nut and union shoulders provide clamping and retention. The gasket is
compressed between the designed faces and should remain centered during
assembly.

## What actually seals

The flat gasket seals the interface between the specified union faces. A reliable
seal depends on the gasket dimensions and material, face condition, alignment,
and sufficient but controlled compression.

The union nut supplies clamping force through the union halves. Its thread
normally provides mechanical engagement and is not the primary water-sealing
surface in this construction.

## What does not seal

Do not assume that the following seals a flat-gasket union:

- the union nut thread;
- thread tape or paste wrapped around the union thread;
- the outside of the nut;
- a gasket sitting off-center or outside the designed faces;
- a second random washer added to compensate for a wrong gasket;
- additional tightening after the gasket has split, rolled, or extruded.

If the product uses a captive O-ring or conical seat, this unit does not define
that sealing method. Classify and diagnose the actual design instead.

## Permitted movement and tightening

Before tightening, the union halves should align without force and the gasket
should remain seated. The nut should engage smoothly by hand for the initial
turns.

After the faces are seated, the connection should not move in normal service.
Do not use the union nut to pull misaligned pipes together or to set a final
orientation that should have been established elsewhere.

Over-tightening can deform the gasket, damage the faces, distort a valve body,
or make later disassembly difficult. Under-tightening can leave insufficient
compression and allow movement or leakage. Use the product-specific tightening
instruction when available; do not invent a torque value from appearance.

## Typical failures

Typical failure modes include:

- wrong gasket diameter, thickness, material, or profile;
- gasket missing, displaced, cut, hardened, swollen, or permanently flattened;
- damaged, dirty, corroded, or scratched sealing faces;
- union halves tilted or not fully seated;
- pipe stress, vibration, or thermal movement loading the union;
- cross-threaded, stripped, or damaged union nut threads;
- under-tightening or over-tightening;
- incorrect assembly order or a missing centering element;
- a nearby leak mistaken for a union-face leak.

Replacing the gasket alone may not correct a damaged face, misalignment, or
unsupported pipe.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms include:

- a drip or seepage around the union seam;
- leakage that appears after the union has been disconnected and reassembled;
- leakage that changes when the nut is tightened or the pipe is supported;
- a gasket visible outside the face or pinched at one side;
- a nut that feels tight while the union faces remain visibly uneven;
- water tracking along the underside of the pipe or valve body.

The first wet point must be identified before concluding that the union gasket
is the source.

## Diagnosis

When safe isolation and depressurization are available:

1. Isolate the relevant branch and allow hot parts to cool.
2. Dry the union and nearby joints, then observe the first wet point under the
   lowest safe test condition.
3. Check whether pipe support or movement changes the symptom without adding
   tightening force.
4. Inspect the union from the outside for tilted faces, a displaced gasket,
   cross-threading, cracks, and evidence of over-tightening.
5. If disassembly is justified, preserve the assembly order and inspect both
   faces, the gasket, the nut, and the alignment of the pipework.
6. Compare the replacement gasket with the original and with the manufacturer’s
   specification; visual similarity is not sufficient.

If a gasket is not clearly present, do not add one at random. The fitting may
use an O-ring, conical seat, captive seal, or a different union geometry.

## Assembly errors

Common errors include:

- placing the gasket on the wrong side or outside the sealing faces;
- using a gasket with the wrong inside or outside diameter;
- assembling with dirt, old gasket fragments, or corrosion on the faces;
- pinching, folding, or displacing the gasket while bringing the halves
  together;
- starting the nut at an angle or forcing a cross-thread;
- using the union to pull unsupported or misaligned pipework into position;
- adding thread sealant to the union thread;
- tightening until leakage changes instead of correcting the seating;
- reusing a gasket that has taken a permanent compression set.

## Safety

Isolate and depressurize the relevant water or heating branch before loosening
the union. Expect trapped water inside the component and protect nearby
electrical equipment and finished surfaces from discharge.

Stop and escalate when isolation cannot be verified, the joint is hot or under
significant pressure, a shared riser or central-heating circuit is involved,
the fitting is cracked, or the joint could cause uncontrolled flooding.
