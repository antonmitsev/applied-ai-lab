# Axial (Face) Sealing with an O-Ring

> Draft canonical unit. This unit covers an O-ring compressed between opposing
> faces. It does not define radial sealing in a bore or around a spigot.

## What it is

An axial, or face, O-ring seal is installed in a groove on a flat or nearly
flat face. When the mating face is clamped against it, the O-ring is compressed
in the direction normal to the faces and forms a barrier around the fluid path.

The groove, mating face, clamping arrangement, and pressure direction must be
treated as one design. An O-ring that looks suitable for a radial groove may be
wrong for an axial groove.

## Where it is used

Axial O-ring seals may be used between flanges, valve covers, cartridge ends,
inspection covers, housings, and component faces designed to close against a
grooved sealing surface.

Some products use a flat gasket, a molded face seal, a metal seat, or a captive
O-ring in a similar location. The visible presence of a circular groove does
not by itself establish the intended seal type.

## How it seals

The O-ring is compressed axially between the groove base or walls and the mating
face. Its elastic recovery maintains contact while the clamping arrangement
holds the faces together.

Pressure direction matters. Depending on the design, pressure may tend to move
the O-ring toward one side of the groove or toward a clearance gap. Groove
geometry, available support, surface condition, and the pressure range must be
verified for the actual component.

Mechanical retention comes from bolts, a clamp, a threaded cover, a union
arrangement, a shoulder, or another designed clamping feature. The O-ring
should not be expected to hold the parts together.

## Critical dimensions

The following dimensions and conditions must be considered together:

- O-ring inside diameter and cross-section;
- groove inside and outside diameter, depth, width, and corner radii;
- mating-face flatness, parallelism, and surface condition;
- designed axial compression and available gland volume;
- clearance gaps relative to pressure and material hardness;
- pressure direction, temperature, and any relative movement;
- bolt, clamp, or cover loading and its distribution around the face.

Do not identify the correct ring from the groove diameter alone. A face seal can
leak when the ring is the right apparent diameter but the cross-section,
groove depth, compression, face flatness, or clamping sequence is wrong.

Case-specific measurements must remain tied to the case, measurement method,
and uncertainty. They must not be promoted to a universal O-ring designation.

## Materials

The elastomer must be compatible with the actual fluid, temperature, pressure,
cleaning agents, and service duration. EPDM and NBR are common families in
plumbing markets, but the choice is application-specific and compounds are not
interchangeable by name alone.

Where a cover or flange is part of a potable-water, heating, or chemically
treated system, use the component manufacturer’s material specification or a
reviewed compatibility source. Do not infer approval from color or hardness
alone.

## What correct installation looks like

A correctly installed axial O-ring should be:

- the specified size, cross-section, material, and hardness for the design;
- seated continuously in the correct face groove;
- free of cuts, twists, pinches, debris, and previous compression damage;
- installed on clean, flat, and aligned sealing faces;
- exposed to a compatible lubricant only when the design or manufacturer
  permits it;
- compressed by an even clamping sequence rather than by one-sided force;
- protected from being squeezed into an unintended clearance gap.

Bring the mating faces together without sliding one face across the ring. If
the ring repeatedly falls out, moves, or cannot remain centered, investigate
the groove, retaining method, orientation, or missing component rather than
using sealant to hold it in place.

## Typical failures

Typical failure modes include:

- wrong O-ring cross-section or diameter;
- ring twisted, pinched, cut, flattened, hardened, or swollen;
- groove too shallow, too deep, contaminated, or damaged;
- rough, scratched, warped, or non-parallel mating face;
- uneven bolt or clamp loading;
- insufficient clamping or excessive compression;
- extrusion into a clearance gap under pressure;
- thermal movement or pressure cycling that unloads the face;
- chemical or thermal incompatibility;
- missing cover, retainer, guide, or locating feature.

## Signs of an incorrect size

The following observations increase suspicion of an incorrect size or geometry,
but do not prove it:

- the ring sits loose, proud, or below the intended groove position;
- the ring cannot be seated continuously around the groove;
- the ring is pinched or displaced when the faces are brought together;
- the cover rocks or closes unevenly before clamping;
- the ring shows a narrow damaged band, extrusion, or one-sided flattening;
- leakage occurs at one face sector rather than uniformly around the seal;
- the seal works briefly after tightening and then leaks again under pressure or
  temperature change.

Confirm the groove, face, clamping, and pressure conditions before selecting a
different ring.

## Compatibility

Compatibility includes the process fluid and all relevant operating conditions:
temperature, pressure, water treatment, antifreeze, cleaning products, oils,
and exposure time. It also includes compatibility with the mating component and
any lubricant used during assembly.

The assistant must not claim that a specific O-ring material is approved for a
named product or fluid without a reviewed source covering that exact scope. If
the model, medium, or operating conditions are unknown, ask for them or direct
the user to the component documentation.

## Safety

Before opening a face-sealed cover or flange, isolate the system and remove
stored pressure and heat. A face seal may retain water even when the visible
pipe is empty.

Stop and escalate when the cover is part of a pressurized vessel, water heater,
central-heating circuit, or inaccessible system; when the isolation state is
uncertain; or when a failed seal could release hot water or cause flooding.
